UNIT 0110–15 minutes

What is left behind?

Space missions leave more than photographs and discoveries. Some hardware remains where it completed its work; other spacecraft continue in orbit or end their missions in different ways.

Learning goals

  • Explain why evidence and sources matter.
  • Describe one question a researcher should ask about an artifact.

Help & orientation

Frequently Asked Questions

Learn how to use the catalog, interpret map markers, and understand what our evidence labels mean.

What is Abandoned but Not Forgotten?

An educational catalog exploring NASA Moon and Mars mission hardware and the scientific legacy of missions. The goal is to explain what is known, what remains, and where evidence comes from.

Does “abandoned” mean every object is still on a planetary surface?

No. Objects can be surface hardware, orbiters, impactors, or spacecraft with different end-of-mission outcomes. Check each record’s destination, mission, location, and status rather than assuming all hardware is on the ground.

How should I read a map marker?

Select a marker or a record to see the associated object and mission. Coordinates and their accuracy should be interpreted as recorded; an orbital or approximate location should not be presented as a precise surface site.

Why do some fields say “Unknown” or “Not verified”?

We avoid inventing values. When the sources available to the project do not support a detail, the interface should mark it as unknown or under verification until reliable evidence is found.

Can teachers use the website in class?

Yes. The Space Classroom offers short lesson outlines, discussion prompts, and activities. Teachers can adapt them to the learners’ age and curriculum and should verify source material before formal assessment.

How do I compare two pieces of equipment?

Open Compare, choose the records, and review their fields side by side. Missing values should remain visible as unknown instead of being treated as a match.

Where does the information come from?

Use the source links attached to individual records and the Sources & Evidence Register. Prefer official NASA mission pages, NASA technical material, and JPL mission archives where applicable.

How can I report an error?

Use the site’s feedback/correction control if available, and include the record name, the claim in question, a reliable source, and the correction you recommend.

Core Research Rules & Scope Constraints

  • NASA-Led Missions Only: Hardware is strictly included if NASA led or operated the host mission. Joint partner instruments on non-NASA missions do not qualify the host spacecraft.
  • No Fabricated Values: Missing landing coordinates, launch dates, or image assets are labeled explicitly as Unknown / Not verified rather than estimated.
  • Distinct Milestones: Launch, orbital insertion, closest approach, touchdown, last communication, and formal mission termination are kept strictly distinct.
  • Stable Internal IDs: Identifier prefixes (e.g. M001, R001) are project-internal anchors, never misrepresented as official NASA catalog numbers.

About Abandoned but Not Forgotten

Conceived for the NASA Space Apps Challenge, this initiative brings extraterrestrial archaeology into public focus through evidence-grounded interactive exploration.

Why Extraterrestrial Archaeology?

When Apollo 11 lifted off from Mare Tranquillitatis on July 21, 1969, the descent stage remained behind as humanity's very first extraterrestrial monument. Over subsequent decades, dozens of robotic probes, stationary landers, rovers, and orbiters have completed their missions across the solar system.

These machines represent the pinnacle of engineering in their era. Rather than space debris, they are historical artifacts of humanity's initial expansion into the cosmos.

Compliance & Scope Governance

Every catalog record adheres to the October 2026 PRD v1.0 specifications. Non-NASA missions (such as Soviet Luna/Lunokhod and ISRO/ESA solo missions) were formally excluded to ensure clean institutional accountability.

Images are attributed directly to original NASA archival entries with verified provenance.
