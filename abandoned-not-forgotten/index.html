Sources & Evidence Register

Master register of primary archival sources, data repositories, and research rules governing the Abandoned but Not Forgotten catalog.

Core Research Rules & Scope Constraints

  • NASA-Led Missions Only: Hardware is strictly included if NASA led or operated the host mission. Joint partner instruments on non-NASA missions do not qualify the host spacecraft.
  • No Fabricated Values: Missing landing coordinates, launch dates, or image assets are labeled explicitly as Unknown / Not verified rather than estimated.
  • Distinct Milestones: Launch, orbital insertion, closest approach, touchdown, last communication, and formal mission termination are kept strictly distinct.
  • Stable Internal IDs: Identifier prefixes (e.g. M001, R001) are project-internal anchors, never misrepresented as official NASA catalog numbers.

About Abandoned but Not Forgotten

Conceived for the NASA Space Apps Challenge, this initiative brings extraterrestrial archaeology into public focus through evidence-grounded interactive exploration.

Why Extraterrestrial Archaeology?

When Apollo 11 lifted off from Mare Tranquillitatis on July 21, 1969, the descent stage remained behind as humanity's very first extraterrestrial monument. Over subsequent decades, dozens of robotic probes, stationary landers, rovers, and orbiters have completed their missions across the solar system.

These machines represent the pinnacle of engineering in their era. Rather than space debris, they are historical artifacts of humanity's initial expansion into the cosmos.

Compliance & Scope Governance

Every catalog record adheres to the October 2026 PRD v1.0 specifications. Non-NASA missions (such as Soviet Luna/Lunokhod and ISRO/ESA solo missions) were formally excluded to ensure clean institutional accountability.

Images are attributed directly to original NASA archival entries with verified provenance.
